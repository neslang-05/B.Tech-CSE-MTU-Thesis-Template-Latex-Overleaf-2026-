\section{Model Performance}
The empirical performance of the proposed deep convolutional neural network framework was rigorously evaluated using standard classification metrics: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1 Score~\cite{mohanty2016}. The quantitative evaluation was executed on an unseen validation test dataset following the completion of a two-phase transfer learning pipeline built upon the ResNet50 architecture~\cite{he2016deep, xu2021}.

The fully converged model demonstrated exceptional discriminatory capability in categorising commercial seed samples into binary quality classifications: \texttt{Pure} (legitimate certified seed stock) and \texttt{Impure} (defective, damaged, or counterfeit seed stock). The resulting performance metrics confirm that the proposed visual inspection framework is highly reliable for real-world agricultural deployment in resource-constrained environments.

\section{Regularization Effectiveness}
Because the empirical training dataset was constrained to 1,563 field images, suppressing parameter overfitting was a paramount architectural priority during network optimization. A multi-layered regularization strategy was engineered to preserve generalization capacity across unseen test distributions.

The relative effectiveness of these architectural interventions was quantified by tracking the divergence between training and validation loss trajectories, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:regularization}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Impact of Architectural Regularization on Model Generalization}
  \label{tab:regularization}
  \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l c X @{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Optimization Configuration} & \textbf{Train-Validation Gap} & \textbf{Empirical Generalization Outcome} \\
    \midrule
    Unregularized Baseline & 25.0\% -- 30.0\% & Severe parameter memorization and high validation divergence \\
    \addlinespace
    Standard Dropout Only & 8.0\% -- 12.0\% & Moderate stabilization with residual overfitting on complex textures \\
    \addlinespace
    Final Combined Strategy & 2.8\% & Exceptional generalization with tight convergence across epochs ($<5\%$ gap) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The final train-validation accuracy gap of 2.8\% confirms that the regularized ResNet50 framework successfully avoids overfitting while maintaining stable convergence dynamics. This tight performance envelope was achieved through the simultaneous orchestration of the following techniques:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Weight Decay (\texttt{AdamW}):} Applied an L2 penalty of $1 \times 10^{-4}$ directly to weight parameters rather than gradients, preventing parameter inflation during adaptive optimization~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}.
    \item \textbf{Label Smoothing Regularization:} Set smoothing parameter $\alpha = 0.1$, discouraging the network from forming overconfident logits on noisy training labels~\cite{muller2019label}.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Dropout and Batch Normalization:} Integrated intermediate dropout layers ($p = 0.5$) before dense projection heads alongside batch-wise feature normalization~\cite{he2016deep}.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Learning Rate Scheduling:} Implemented cosine annealing with warm restarts, allowing the network to escape suboptimal local minima during fine-tuning.
    \item \textbf{Aggressive Data Augmentation:} Enforced random affine rotations, horizontal flips, Gaussian blur, and color jitter to simulate real-world field capture variances.
\end{itemize}

As documented in Table~\ref{tab:modelcomparison} and illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:model-comparison}, ResNet50 achieved the highest overall diagnostic performance across all key classification metrics. The network's identity shortcut connections allow gradients to flow unimpeded across 50 deep convolutional layers, overcoming vanishing gradient degradation during fine-tuning~\cite{he2016deep, xu2021}. 

While MobileNetV2 demonstrated rapid training convergence (8.5 minutes) and reduced memory footprint, its classification accuracy (99.13\%) was slightly lower. DenseNet121 and GoogleNet provided identical peak diagnostic capability (99.57\%), but ResNet50 exhibited superior numerical stability and seamless compatibility with production deployment tools such as ONNX Runtime and TensorRT~\cite{pytorch2019}. Consequently, ResNet50 was chosen as the optimal balance between diagnostic precision and computational efficiency for the AgriVerify backend microservice.

\section{Challenges Encountered and Mitigation Strategies}
\label{sec:challenges}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Several technical, computational, and operational challenges were encountered during the engineering and deployment of the AgriVerify architecture. 

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Constrained Empirical Dataset Size:} The primary dataset comprised 1,563 field images, posing a substantial risk of over-parameterization during deep learning optimization. This challenge was resolved by adopting a two-phase transfer learning schedule on pre-trained ImageNet weights~\cite{krizhevsky2012imagenet}, coupled with heavy stochastic data augmentation and weight decay regularization~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}.
    \item \textbf{High Visual Similarity Across Defective Classes:} Discriminating between mechanically damaged grains and sophisticated counterfeit seed stock proved highly complex due to overlapping morphological features. Incorporating multi-modal OCR text extraction alongside visual feature maps significantly improved classification accuracy on ambiguous packaging~\cite{mohanty2016}.
    \item \textbf{High Field Lighting Variability:} Rural image capture via low-end mobile camera sensors introduced severe illumination variances, lens distortion, and motion blur. Client-side EXIF orientation normalization and automated contrast adjustments within the Next.js proxy layer stabilized input tensor distributions before inference execution.
    \item \textbf{Computational Model Weight Footprint:} The 50-layer ResNet architecture introduced significant model parameter storage requirements ($\sim$100 MB checkpoint size), complicating lightweight serverless container deployment. Utilizing PyTorch JIT compilation and half-precision (\texttt{FP16}) quantization reduced memory overhead and accelerated inference throughput without degrading diagnostic accuracy~\cite{pytorch2019}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary and System Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This chapter presented a comprehensive empirical evaluation of the AgriVerify platform, verifying the deep learning model, backend microservice responsiveness, and end-to-end user workflows. The regularized ResNet50 classifier achieved an exceptional overall test accuracy of 99.57\% and an F1 Score of 99.57\%, demonstrating robust discriminatory performance across pure (genuine) and impure (defective/counterfeit) seed categories. Concurrent load testing confirmed that the ASP.NET Core backend and Next.js proxy layer maintain stable, low-latency execution under simulated agricultural peak traffic conditions. Furthermore, rigorous functional testing validated the complete suite of user interfaces, confirming intuitive operation across the farmer verification portals, grievance tracking queues, and administrative oversight dashboards.

Despite these strong empirical outcomes, certain limitations remain within the current implementation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The model is currently trained exclusively on paddy seed imagery; extending support to other staple crops (e.g., wheat, maize, soybean) requires expanded multi-crop dataset curation~\cite{xu2021}.
    \item Automated mobile edge inference (offline classification on smartphones via ONNX Runtime Mobile) has not yet been implemented, requiring active cellular connectivity for cloud inference.
    \item Finer-grained phytopathological disease detection beyond broad physical quality grading is not currently supported by the classification ontology~\cite{mohanty2016}.
\end{itemize}

Addressing these limitations through mobile quantization, multi-crop dataset expansion, and explainable AI heatmaps represents highly promising avenues for future agricultural research and platform development.
